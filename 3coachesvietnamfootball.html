Alfred Riedl
1998 – 2007
01

Alfred Riedl

Austrian Head Coach · 3 stints with the national team

Barely months into his first tenure in 1998, Austrian coach Alfred Riedl had already seen enough. Vietnamese football administrators were obsessed with national team results — chasing trophies while neglecting the youth development that every serious football nation builds from the ground up.

"Vietnamese football is building a house from the roof down."

Brutal. Accurate. Timeless. He led Vietnam to a historic 3–0 win over Thailand at Tiger Cup 1998 — a result that shattered the psychological barrier Vietnamese football had against its Southeast Asian rival for decades — and guided the team to the quarterfinals of the 2007 Asian Cup.

Riedl carried a kidney donated by a Vietnamese fan and called Vietnam his second home. It took over 20 years — and the rise of youth academies like HAGL, PVF, and Viettel — before his warning was finally heeded. He passed away in 2020, aged 70.

Karl-Heinz Weigang
1966 · 1995 – 1997
02

Karl-Heinz Weigang

German Coach · The trailblazer who came twice

Here's the detail most people miss: Weigang first arrived in Saigon not as a football coach, but as a technical teacher at Cao Thang Engineering School. Yet in 1966, he led the South Vietnam national team — featuring legends like Phạm Huỳnh Tam Lang, Đỗ Thới Vinh, and Nguyễn Văn Mộng — to win the prestigious Merdeka Cup. It would take Vietnamese football over 40 years to match that achievement.

Twenty-nine years later, he returned. Armed with the German football philosophy of iron discipline and physical conditioning, he transformed the golden generation of Huỳnh Đức, Hồng Sơn, and Hữu Thắng, delivering Vietnam's first SEA Games silver medal in the unified era.

"How much did you sell that match for?" — Weigang confronting his own players to their faces after a suspicious draw against Laos at Tiger Cup 1996.

He was 81 when he passed away in 2017, still coaching in the Malaysian league. A football man to the very end.

Phạm Huỳnh Tam Lang
1942 – 2014
03

Phạm Huỳnh Tam Lang

Player & Coach · A life lived across two Vietnams

Few lives in Vietnamese football carry the weight of history the way Tam Lang's does. His father was a Viet Minh fighter killed by French colonial forces — yet Tam Lang himself served as a police officer under the South Vietnamese government. Then 1975 came, and the country changed around him entirely. He kept playing. He kept coaching. He became one of the first athletes from the old regime to be admitted into the Communist Party.

In 1966, he captained South Vietnam to the Merdeka Cup title and became the first Vietnamese player ever selected for the "Asian All-Star" team. International media called him the Golden Arrow. He went on to coach Cảng Sài Gòn for 20 years, winning 4 national championships.

"Playing football is learning to be a person — not learning to kick people."

He passed away in 2014. His own words about his life were quietly devastating: "My fate has been far more bitter than the glory people imagine." But the dignity with which he carried that fate made him one of the most beloved figures in Vietnamese football history.
